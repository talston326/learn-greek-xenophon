# Xenophon’s Apology — Complete Interactive Reader

## Published scope

The optional course resource now presents all 34 numbered sections of Xenophon’s *Apology* on one continuous page. It is available from Resources and the optional Lessons panel without course prerequisites.

- Greek and Interlinear retain the edition’s printed order. Gray section numbers precede the text; there are no sentence headings or editorial section titles.
- Reading order shows all the same Greek tokens and glosses in an editorial study sequence. Every section has short phrase groups with English cues beneath them; §§2–34 also retain a full English guide below the section. Switch to Greek or Interlinear for source order.
- English shows the complete translation, one paragraph per section.
- Clicking a word opens its lemma, lexical gloss, morphology, syntax note, and related words. On narrow screens the analysis opens as a closable panel near the selected word.
- The section links are shortcuts within the continuous page. Reading progress stays in this device’s browser storage. The reader makes no grade, XP, or lesson-completion request.

## Sources and editorial status

The Greek follows [E. C. Marchant’s 1921 edition at Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0211). The token and initial morphology data for §§2–34 derive from [Open Greek Corpus annotations](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ciscoriordan/open-greek-corpus-annotations), CC BY-SA 4.0. Brief lexical definitions for §§11–34 draw on [Perseus and Logeion short definitions](https://github.com/helmadik/shortdefs), edited for this reader. The English for §§11–34 draws on [O. J. Todd’s 1923 translation at Perseus](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/library/urn%3Acts%3AgreekLit%3Atlg0032.tlg005.perseus-eng2/), excluding source footnotes; §§22–23 were edited to align the translation with the Greek section break. §§1–10 retain the course draft translation. Marchant’s bracketed editorial supplement in §10 is displayed as such.

§1 remains the hand-annotated prototype. §§2–10 retain their provisional course annotations. The new §§2–34 phrase arrangements and English cues are editorial study aids, while the new §§11–34 syntax links and lexical definitions may need more context-specific revision. An Ancient Greek instructor should review the morphology, glosses, syntax, and translation before treating this as a scholarly edition.

The banner is an imaginative, historically informed reconstruction of Socrates before a large Athenian jury, with a single-story stoa in the Agora background. The precise trial venue is uncertain.
