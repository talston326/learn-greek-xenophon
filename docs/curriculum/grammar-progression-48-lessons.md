## Purpose and source boundary

This is the project's resource for **what grammar each numbered lesson teaches**. It follows the order of grammar topics in the supplied *Athenaze* table of contents while retaining what has already been taught in Lessons 1–5. Story subjects, characters, and titles may change without changing the grammar map.

*Athenaze* has 16 chapters in Book I and 14 in Book II. The **48-lesson allocation is our curriculum design**, not a textbook syllabus: Book I occupies Lessons 1–32 at one chapter half per lesson; Book II occupies Lessons 33–48 at one chapter per lesson, except Chapters 21 and 25 are each split across two lessons. Chapter 20 uses the textbook's unusual γ/δ labels. Unit 0 covers the alphabet, pronunciation, breathings, keyboarding, and decoding before Lesson 1.

The supplied PDF contains contents pages rather than full grammar explanations. Its chapter and section headings establish **order and coverage**, not the wording of our lessons or a claim that a learner has mastered a topic. The excerpt does not identify its edition, so this document does not assume one. Our explanations, readings, and exercises must be independently written.

## Allocation and pacing

- Lessons 1–32 follow Book I chapter halves α then β. The first five rows are a **crosswalk** to completed lessons, with the catch-up moves above; they are not a proposal to rewrite those lessons.
- Lessons 33–48 follow Book II chapter order. Chapters 21 and 25 each occupy two lessons, leaving 16 lessons for Chapters 17–30.
- A lesson is a learning unit, not necessarily one class meeting. Book II chapters, especially 18–20, 23, and 26–28, need multiple explanations and practice sets inside their assigned lesson.
- Reviews at Lessons 12, 24, 36, and 48 are cumulative additions; they do not replace the listed new grammar.
- The compact topic labels below summarize the PDF headings. At authoring time, check **every grammar heading in the assigned chapter or chapter half**; a short table cell is never an omission license.

Textbook references below are grammar references only. The status column separates published lessons from the future plan. Reading themes remain in the separate [Curriculum Narrative](curriculum-narrative.md).

## Handoff after Lesson 5

Lesson 6 develops **I 3β: plural article, adjective, and noun cases plus accent shifting**. Lesson 4 supplied the singular-case and accent base; Lesson 5 supplied plural verb subjects and forms. The completed Lesson 6 reading uses friends, athletes, and Athenians to make plural cases meaningful. Its Language Study teaches the plural patterns with practice and final grammar exercises; its culture page discusses the Greek gymnasium and leads to the final quiz.

Continue in the PDF's order after Lesson 6. When a topic has appeared early, distinguish that appearance from the later chapter's full paradigm or range of uses. In particular, Lesson 3's middle/passive introduction does not exhaust I 6α–β or I 16α, and its demonstratives do not exhaust I 14β. The same principle applies to infinitives introduced in Lesson 3 and taught more systematically in Lesson 5.

## Rules for changing a reading

1. Keep the assigned Athenaze reference and formal grammar objectives fixed unless the curriculum is explicitly revised.
2. Write the new story primarily with grammar already taught plus the current lesson's targets.
3. Label constructions outside the taught objectives as supported reading exposure. Supply a clear gloss or reading note; do not test independent production of the full construction yet.
4. Distinguish required new vocabulary, review vocabulary, and words supplied only for comprehension. Story revisions may change vocabulary without moving grammar objectives.
5. Preserve independently written explanations and exercises. Borrow the sequence, not Athenaze's prose, stories, or exercise banks.
6. After a revision, check the Greek, translation, glosses, vocabulary, grammar examples, exercises, and quiz together.
7. Record a reading revision separately from any change to this grammar map.

## Authoring requirements for each lesson

Every completed lesson should contain:

- **Reading:** the current story, translation, vocabulary, and support for unfamiliar forms.
- **Language study:** objectives expanded from the assigned reference, explanations, paradigms as appropriate, guided examples, and practice.
- **Final page:** cultural context relevant to the current story, cumulative review, and an assessment limited to taught objectives.

At authoring time, maintain a checklist with these fields: lesson number; textbook reference; objective; first formal teaching; prior supported appearances; practice coverage; assessment coverage; completion status. This makes it possible to change a story without losing the progression.

A lesson is complete when its required pages and practice/assessment match the reviewed objectives and the published content matches the reviewed version. For this application, checking the repository alone is insufficient: verify the database-backed lesson served by the live API as well.
