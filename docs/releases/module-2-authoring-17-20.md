## Primary sources checked

- **17, Plato, Laches 190e–192d:** [Jowett public-domain text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1584/pg1584-images.html) and [Horan text with Stephanus markers](https://www.platonicfoundation.org/translation/platos-laches/). Checked the initial post-holding example; fighting during retreat, including Scythians and reported Spartans at Plataea; expansion to illness, poverty, and civic affairs; endurance and judgment. This is Plato’s standalone conversation, with Socrates, Laches, and Nicias in the frame. Xenophon is absent. The selected discussion remains unresolved; the simplified Greek does not reproduce the full original or claim to solve the whole dialogue.
- **18, Xenophon, Anabasis 1.3.1–21:** [Greek and Dakyns translation](https://www.mikrosapoplous.gr/xenophon/anb3d10.htm), with [Brownson 1.3](https://beta.perseus.tufts.edu/urn%3Acts%3AgreekLit%3Atlg0032.tlg006.perseus-eng2%3A1.3/). Checked soldiers’ objection to the service they were hired for, Clearchus’s failed coercion, assembly and tears, public solidarity and private reassurance, the logistical dangers of leaving, the Abrocomas explanation, and the half-again increase from one to one-and-a-half monthly darics. 1.3.21 explicitly distinguishes suspicion of war with the King from an open announcement. The reading preserves that distinction and legitimate consent concerns.
- **19, Xenophon, Anabasis 1.4.11–18:** [Greek and Dakyns translation](https://www.mikrosapoplous.gr/xenophon/anb3d10.htm). Checked disclosure at Thapsacus, soldiers’ accusations about their commanders’ prior knowledge (reported accusations, not independent proof), promised five minas at Babylon, full wages until return to Ionia, the crossing, and the inhabitants’ reported interpretation of the ford. Their supposed divine sign is labeled reported interpretation, not verified miracle.
- **19, Xenophon, Anabasis 1.7.3–9:** [Greek and Dakyns translation](https://www.mikrosapoplous.gr/xenophon/anb7d10.htm). Checked praise of Greek freedom, enemy numbers and noise, Gaulites’s doubts about fulfillment, rewards including golden crowns dependent on success, and advice that Cyrus remain behind rather than fight personally. This later pre-battle scene is explicitly separated from Thapsacus. Simplified futures are teaching adaptations, and rewards are promises rather than narrated fulfillment.
- **20, Xenophon, Hellenica 2.4.19–22:** [Greek/Brownson reading interface](https://www.magalia.wiki/historiae/fulltext/xenophon-hellenica.html) and [Dakyns Book II, chapter 4](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1174/1174-h/1174-h.htm). Checked recovery of dead and cross-party conversation, Cleocritus’s religious herald role and fine voice, shared rites/festivals/choral activity/education/military service, kinship and marriage, shared grief, opposition to the Thirty, and officials leading men away. The scene is 403 BCE, before the 401 expedition; the web interface’s broad “404 BCE” section labels are unsuitable for this episode. No immediate reconciliation or Xenophon attendance is invented. Future intentions and purpose participles are labeled course additions.

## Greek and question review

- Lessons 17–18 use present retelling; 19–20 introduce future statements. No formal past or subjunctive teaching is added. Laches’s short nominal definition uses unaccented indefinite τις without a following enclitic copula, so its form stays visibly distinct from interrogative τίς. Advanced infinitives, irregular verbs, comparative wording, contracted forms, names, and construction phrases are supported by exact-substring blue glosses.
- Lesson 17 includes active participial formation in all genders, the complete singular/plural declension, agreement, attributive/substantival/circumstantial uses, middle contrasts, and ongoing simultaneous action.
- Lesson 18 covers -ντ- nominative/dative changes; complete πόλις, ἄστυ, βασιλεύς, ναῦς, and βοῦς tables; possessive/relational/partitive/prepositional genitives and time-within-which contrast; article substantivization, groups, names, and attributive/predicate position. Ships/cattle and γέρων/γίγας are supplementary examples, not forced historical incidents. The γέρων stem question was revised to avoid having two valid stem-revealing answers. The ambassadors entry uses the Attic plural πρέσβεις, πρέσβεων, οἱ, avoiding the singular πρέσβυς’s usual “old man” sense.
- Lesson 19 distinguishes ordinary sigmatic endings and consonant changes, -ίζω asigmatic futures, sigmatic futures of contract verbs, deponent/middle futures, and promise/intention/prediction. All assessed hearing futures use middle forms: ἀκούσομαι and ἀκουσόμεθα. Irregular δώσω, παρέξω, λήψεται and contracted μαχοῦνται are glossed; the supplied forms are not guessed from the present.
- Lesson 20 distinguishes liquid/nasal contracted futures and accents; εἶμι versus εἰμί including shared εἶ; future purpose versus present ongoing participles; δεῖ/χρή with infinitives and accusative actors; direct questions, interrogative versus indefinite accents, and question words.
- Noun dictionary entries include genitive and gender. Vocabulary distractor construction excludes overlapping English senses, including πόλις/ἄστυ and noun/verb “promise.” Reverse promise prompts explicitly distinguish noun and verb. Correct positions cycle evenly. Future-wording and source-boundary interpretation are assessed alongside morphology.
- Automatic generator audits check at least six exact-substring glosses per reading paragraph, unique question prompts within pools, four distinct choices, a single marked answer, position balance, and exactly 30 final questions. `node --check` passed. Two successive generations produced identical content hashes. Independent peer review checked the paradigms, future-purpose forms, source framing, and required grammar question keys; its indefinite-accent and ambassadors-entry suggestions were incorporated.
