Module 1 Review · Wisdom and Socrates

Revisit Lessons 1–12 before the exam. Use the refresher exercises as often as you like; they do not affect your grade. The exam requires 28 correct answers out of 40 (70%) to enter Module 2.

Lessons 1–3 · Home, household, and education

  • Lesson 1: Find the subject and object; match articles and adjectives; read present active verbs. Xenophon tends the horse at home in a reconstructed scene.
    ὁ Ξενοφῶν · τὸν ἵππον θεραπεύει
  • Lesson 2: Read the household roles, εἰμί, possession with the genitive, and simple place phrases. Notice the women’s weaving work.
    ἡ οἰκία τοῦ Γρύλλου · ἐν τῷ ἀγρῷ
  • Lesson 3: Read third-person singular and plural verbs, direct objects, infinitives, demonstratives, and introductory middle forms.
    οἱ παῖδες μανθάνουσιν · γράφειν

Lessons 4–6 · Care, questions, and training

  • Lesson 4: Review singular cases, adjective agreement, and accent clues. Gryllus’s departure scene is a labeled reconstruction.
    τῷ καλῷ ἵππῳ · τῆς λόγχης
  • Lesson 5: Use third-person plurals, singular and plural commands, infinitives, and proclitics. The later narrow-lane tradition is expanded for the course.
    φέρουσιν · φέρετε · μένειν
  • Lesson 6: Identify plural cases and shifting accents. In Memorabilia 3.12, Socrates addresses Epigenes about bodily training.
    οἱ φίλοι · τοὺς φίλους · τῶν φίλων

Lessons 7–9 · Procession, work, and friendship

  • Lesson 7: Review all present active persons and first-declension feminine forms. Myrrhine’s individual words are course reconstruction.
    βλέπω · βλέπομεν · αἱ πομπαί
  • Lesson 8: Match noun and adjective forms and distinguish adjectives from adverbs. Xenophon reports women’s textile work, but not their individual dialogue.
    τὰ καλὰ ἱμάτια · καλῶς ὑφαίνει
  • Lesson 9: Read alpha-contract verbs and elision. Compare useful help, loyalty, and character in the friendship discussion.
    τιμῶ · τιμᾷς · ἀλλ᾽

Lessons 10–12 · Decision, Delphi, and departure

  • Lesson 10: Review personal and possessive pronouns, adjective position, and Proxenus’s invitation to meet Cyrus.
    ἐγώ · με · ἡ ἐμὴ ὁδός
  • Lesson 11: Read middle forms and common deponents. Xenophon asks Apollo which gods to honor for a journey he has already chosen.
    πορεύομαι · πορεύονται · βούλομαι
  • Lesson 12: Distinguish whether to go from how to go; identify datives and prepositions. Xenophon reaches Proxenus and Cyrus at Sardis.
    πότερον … ἢ · σὺν τῷ Προξένῳ
