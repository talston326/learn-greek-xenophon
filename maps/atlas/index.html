Ancient Greece · A reading atlas

A map that unfolds as you read.

The Home view covers mainland Greece, Macedonia and Thrace, the Aegean, Crete, western Asia Minor, and the Hellespont and Propontis.

Historical scope: this edition is curated for approximately the fifth century BCE, including Amphipolis (437 BCE) and the unified city of Rhodes (408/407 BCE). It is not yet a passage-by-passage concordance to Thucydides and the Hellenica. A dated ancient name does not by itself prove a mention in either work.

Greek names: attested Ancient Greek where available, with editorial nominative display forms documented separately. A † means a Greek form is missing. Use “Only places with Greek names” for a fully Greek-labelled place layer.

Geography: Detailed shaded terrain loads online as you zoom in, with local Natural Earth relief as a fallback when offline. Detailed physical coastlines include small islands such as Delos. Natural land colors use modern satellite-derived land cover and do not reconstruct ancient vegetation. Close zoom adds landscape and place detail; it does not turn this into a surveyed archaeological plan. Approximate gazetteer positions are shown as hollow dots.

Regions: the optional overlay shows 32 geographical regions and regional groupings used in the Classical Greek world (480–323 BCE), with generalized boundaries. Political control varied; these are not fixed state borders. Northern subdivisions are grouped; Aeniania and Oetaea share one outline. Coverage is concentrated on mainland Greece, Crete and the Greek coastal regions of western Asia Minor.

Region boundaries adapted, georeferenced and simplified from Anaxicrates’s map, translated by UndercoverClassicist (CC BY 4.0), aligned with the atlas coastline. Regional context: Regions of ancient Greece. Messenia remains a geographical region across changes in Spartan control; Cynuria is included with Laconia.

Terrain sources: Mapzen Terrain Tiles on AWS, accessed 30 September 2026.
