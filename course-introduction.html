Introduction

How to Use This Course

This course teaches Ancient Greek as a language for reading, judgment, memory, and conversation with the ancient world. Xenophon gives us a practical guide: clear prose, vivid scenes, Socrates, soldiers, households, cities, virtues, and choices.

Ξ

Why This Course

Learning Greek Through Meaning

The aim is not to memorize disconnected forms and then someday begin reading. From the beginning, you will connect letters, sounds, vocabulary, endings, syntax, and cultural context to readable Greek. Grammar matters because it helps you see what a sentence is doing.

Xenophon is especially useful for this kind of learning. His Greek is orderly enough for beginners to enter, but rich enough to raise real questions about education, courage, self-control, justice, leadership, friendship, and the examined life.

How Lessons Work

A Normal Lesson Path

1. Reading

Begin with the Greek passage. Read slowly, listen where audio is available, use the glosses, and try to notice repeated forms before reaching for an explanation.

2. Vocabulary

Vocabulary is introduced as course data, not as a separate outside list. The vocabulary block, flashcards, mobile review, and practice tools all draw from the same internal words.

3. Language Study

Grammar is gathered in the Language Study page. It explains the forms that help unlock the reading, with emphasis on case, agreement, verb endings, sentence structure, and eventually clauses and discourse.

4. Greek World and Review

Each lesson closes by connecting the language to Greek culture, Xenophon, or the course's virtue framework, then asks you to review and complete the quiz or mastery check.

Tools

Practice, Flashcards, and the Keyboard

Flashcards help you review Greek words from the lessons you have reached. Use them in short sessions, especially for words that feel fragile. On mobile, the course offers compact review tools for today's vocabulary, lesson flashcards, weak words, audio practice, and quick drills.

When an exercise asks you to type Greek, use the Show Keyboard button beside the answer field. The popup keyboard lets you enter Greek letters, breathings, accents, iota subscript, punctuation, spaces, and backspace without changing your system keyboard. You can drag it on larger screens; on smaller screens it docks near the bottom.

Assessment

Practice Sessions and Tests

Practice sessions are meant to teach as well as measure. Read the feedback after each answer, continue through the question stack, and use mistakes as a map for what to review next.

Tests and mastery checks confirm that you are ready to move on. During the build phase, lessons may be easier to open for testing, but quiz completion and mastery behavior still matter for real progress.
